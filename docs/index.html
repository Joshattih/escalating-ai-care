The rules baseline

Three tables, one chain. A rule is a line in an official document that tells the clinician what to look for, how to classify it, or what to do (773; in the repository). A state is a rule that says something about the patient at a moment (259). A statement is a state after cleaning, and it is what the AI doctors rate (257): every statement describes one patient at one moment, in the source's own window, with the source's verdict removed, so that any disagreement recorded is a disagreement between doctors about a patient and not about what a sentence meant. Each statement keeps its row id, document and page, and the people its guideline says it applies to (the "Applies to" column) and the place its guideline was written for (the "Setting" column). Both are columns of statements.csv.

How the rounds work

Twenty-five AI doctors, each played by three models, rate 257 patient statements three times. Every statement gets 75 ratings per round. This section walks through one statement, step by step. The ratings and reasons shown are real answers from the AI doctors.

One statement, one question, 75 doctors: 25 roles, each played by three models.

One model plays all 25 doctors; every doctor rates all 257 statements.

The question put to every doctor:

Must the screening tool stop and hand this patient to a person now?

1  must keep talking
2  keep talking
3  lean keep talking
4  lean hand over
5  hand over
6  must hand over now
IDK  I don't know

There is no middle point. 3 leans one way, 4 the other. IDK is a word off the scale: it is counted, never averaged.

Full size: 257 statements × 25 roles × 3 models = 19,275 calls per round. Each call is a fresh conversation; the model remembers nothing from any other call.

Every doctor answers alone. Nobody sees anybody else.

This is what one doctor receives, word for word. It is put together from three tables. The first three lines are the doctor's role: who they are, who they see, and what they weigh first. The "who they see" line comes from the population named in that doctor's own guideline. The statement comes from the statements on this site, which were cleaned from the rules in the official documents so that it describes one patient at one moment with the document's verdict removed. The question and the scale are the same for everyone. Only the role lines change from doctor to doctor. The links in the prompt below open the list each part comes from.

You are a child and adolescent psychiatrist at an outpatient mental health clinic.
You see children and adolescents referred for assessment, and young adults up to 21.
When deciding whether a screening tool should hand a patient to a person, you weigh suicidal intent and whether there is a plan first.

Statement: The patient says they have had thoughts of suicide in the past few days, including today.

Must the screening tool stop and hand this patient to a person now?

Scale:  1 must keep talking … 6 must hand over now   IDK I don't know

Answer on the first line with exactly one of: 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, or IDK.
On the second line give one sentence of reason.

To keep the example small, three of the 25 roles are shown, D1 to D3, each played by the three models: nine doctors. In the full run every statement has 75. The nine answered:

DoctorRatingReason, one sentence

Sort the ratings, take the middle. When the count is even, keep both middle values.

Why the median and not the mean: if half the doctors say 1 and half say 6, the mean is 3.5, a value no doctor can give. Every rating is then some distance from the group number, even when the group never changes its mind, and that gap would sit under every statement. The median is always a rating somebody gave, so it has no such gap. In the full run the median is over all 75 doctors.

Before round 1, one reason per slot is drawn for every statement. A doctor never sees a reason from its own model family.

A package holds one to five reasons. The reasons written by the reader's own model family are removed, so the number a doctor sees varies from none to five. Reasons are shown without name, model or role; a record is kept of who wrote each one. The random picks are made with a fixed seed, so running the whole thing again picks the same doctors and gives the same packages.
